# Project 15 — Microsoft Teams Meeting and Calendar Troubleshooting

## Project Overview

- **Ticket ID:** TEAMS-002
- **Category:** Microsoft 365 / Microsoft Teams / Outlook Calendar
- **Environment:** Simulated Microsoft 365 work environment
- **Issue:** A meeting scheduled in Outlook does not appear in the user's Teams calendar.
- **Priority:** Time-sensitive, because the user needs to join the meeting shortly.
- **Status:** Simulated troubleshooting exercise completed for learning purposes.

> **Important:** This is a simulated support case created for a learning portfolio. No real user account, mailbox, calendar, or Microsoft 365 tenant was accessed or changed. No Azure resources were created, started, or modified. Azure cost: €0.

## 1. User-Reported Problem

A user reports that a meeting scheduled in Outlook does not appear in the Teams calendar. The user needs to join the meeting shortly.

The support technician must investigate the issue efficiently, preserve the original meeting invitation, and help the user access the meeting if possible.

## 2. Initial Assessment

Before making changes, gather the following information:

- Which work or school account is signed in to Teams?
- Does the meeting appear in Outlook?
- Is the meeting in the expected calendar?
- What date, time, and time zone are shown?
- Is Teams displaying an error or loading indefinitely?
- Is the issue affecting one meeting or multiple meetings?
- Does the issue occur in Teams desktop, Teams on the web, or both?
- Is there a relevant Microsoft 365 service incident?

Because the meeting is time-sensitive, prioritize helping the user access the correct meeting while continuing the investigation.

## 3. Troubleshooting Procedure

### Step 1 — Verify the Account and Meeting

Check that the user is signed in to the correct work or school account.

Open Outlook and confirm that the meeting exists in the expected calendar. Verify its date, time, time zone, organizer, and meeting details.

**Simulated finding:** The meeting appears in Outlook, and the user confirms the correct work account is signed in.

**Action:** Continue investigating the Teams calendar rather than deleting or recreating the meeting.

### Step 2 — Check Teams Loading and Synchronization

Check whether Teams is running normally and whether the calendar appears to be loading correctly.

If appropriate, refresh Teams or close and reopen the application using approved procedures.

**Simulated finding:** Refreshing and restarting Teams does not resolve the issue.

**Action:** Continue with calendar and service checks.

### Step 3 — Check the Calendar and Microsoft 365 Service Status

Verify that the meeting is in the correct calendar and that the user is viewing the expected calendar in Teams.

Check the organization's approved Microsoft 365 service-health information for a relevant incident, if authorized.

**Simulated finding:** The meeting is in the expected calendar, and no relevant service incident is reported in the scenario.

**Action:** Continue with client comparison and evidence gathering.

### Step 4 — Compare Teams Desktop and Teams on the Web

If permitted by organizational policy, open Teams on the web and check whether the meeting appears there.

Compare the result with Teams desktop.

- If the meeting appears on the web but not in the desktop app, investigate a possible desktop-client issue.
- If the meeting is missing in both, collect the relevant details and investigate the account, calendar, or service further.
- Record any error messages and the steps already attempted.

Do not ask the user to disclose passwords or authentication codes. Protect meeting links and other confidential information.

**Simulated finding:** The scenario uses this comparison as a diagnostic step. A real client-specific cause has not been independently established.

### Step 5 — Escalate When Necessary

If the meeting remains missing, document the evidence and escalate through the approved Microsoft 365 support process.

Include:
- The affected account, recorded according to company policy.
- The meeting date and time.
- Whether the meeting appears in Outlook.
- Whether it appears in Teams desktop or Teams on the web.
- Relevant error messages.
- Troubleshooting already completed.
- Any relevant service-health findings.

Avoid deleting the mailbox, resetting the computer, or making unnecessary account changes.

## 4. Time-Sensitive User Support

Because the user needs to join the meeting shortly, ask whether they have the original invitation or an approved meeting link.

If permitted, help the user access the meeting through the original invitation or another verified route provided by the organizer. Confirm the meeting details before joining.

Do not send confidential meeting links to unauthorized recipients or change meeting permissions without approval.

## 5. Verification Before Closing the Ticket

Before resolving the ticket, confirm that:

1. The meeting appears in the expected Teams calendar, or an approved alternative has allowed the user to join.
2. The date, time, time zone, and meeting details are correct.
3. The user can open the meeting and join successfully.
4. Any remaining calendar issue has been documented.
5. The user understands the outcome and any next steps.

A visible meeting entry alone is not sufficient evidence that the user can join successfully.

## 6. Example Troubleshooting Decision Table

| Observation | Possible explanation | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting is missing from Outlook and Teams | Wrong account, calendar, or invitation issue | Verify the account and original invitation |
| Meeting appears in Outlook but not Teams | Calendar display, client, or service issue | Check the selected calendar and Teams status |
| Refreshing Teams does not help | Issue may need further investigation | Compare desktop and web behavior |
| Meeting appears in Teams on the web only | Possible desktop-client issue | Investigate the desktop app using approved steps |
| Meeting is missing in both Teams clients | Possible account, calendar, or service issue | Gather evidence and escalate |
| User needs to join immediately | Time-sensitive access problem | Verify the original invitation and an approved access route |

## 9. Learning Reflection

This exercise demonstrates the importance of checking the user's account, the original Outlook meeting, and the Teams calendar before attempting disruptive changes.

Comparing Teams desktop with Teams on the web can help narrow down where an issue occurs. For time-sensitive problems, a technician should also help the user access the meeting through an approved alternative when possible.
