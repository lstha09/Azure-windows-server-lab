# Project 20 — Full Service Desk Incident Simulation

## Project Overview

**Ticket ID:** SD-020  
**Category:** Microsoft 365 access and security incident  
**Priority:** High — pending assessment under organizational policy  
**Environment:** Simulated Microsoft 365 support scenario  
**Status:** Access restored in the scenario; security investigation remains subject to follow-up  
**Tools:** Simulated Service Desk workflow, Microsoft 365 sign-in troubleshooting concepts, identity verification, MFA security procedures, and incident documentation

> **Important:** This is a fictional training exercise. No real account was accessed or changed. No Azure resources were created, started, or modified. The scenario findings and outcomes are simulated, not evidence of a real incident.

## 1. Incident Description

An employee reported that they could not sign in to Microsoft 365. Outlook was not working, and Microsoft Teams repeatedly requested sign-in.

The employee had an important meeting in 15 minutes. They also reported receiving an unexpected Microsoft Authenticator approval request the previous day.

During the simulated investigation, the employee confirmed that they had not initiated the authentication request and had received two unexpected requests that day.

These reports raised concern about possible unauthorized sign-in attempts or account compromise.

## 2. Business Impact and Priority

The incident potentially affected:

- Access to Outlook email.
- Access to Microsoft Teams.
- Attendance at an important business meeting.
- The security of the employee's Microsoft 365 account.

The combination of an urgent business need and unexpected MFA requests required prompt assessment and coordination with the security team.

The final priority would depend on the organization's incident classification and service-level agreement (SLA).

## 3. Troubleshooting and Investigation

### Step 1 — Assess urgency and gather information

- Confirm the affected user, applications, exact error messages, and when the issue began.
- Ask whether the employee initiated the unexpected Authenticator request.
- Assess the impact on business activities and the upcoming meeting.
- Record the initial facts in the incident ticket.

**Simulated finding:** The employee could not sign in to Microsoft 365 and reported unexpected MFA requests.

### Step 2 — Respond to the security warning

- Tell the employee to deny authentication requests they did not initiate.
- Advise the employee never to share passwords or MFA verification codes.
- Report the suspected security incident through the approved security process.
- Follow the security team's instructions for any containment or recovery actions.

**Simulated finding:** The employee confirmed two unexpected MFA requests that they had not initiated. The issue was treated as a potential account-security incident.

### Step 3 — Investigate sign-in failures safely

After notifying the security team:

- Follow the approved identity-verification procedure.
- Review available sign-in logs, error details, and relevant account information only when authorized.
- Check for suspicious sign-in activity or authentication patterns where the appropriate tools and permissions are available.
- Coordinate findings with the security team.
- Never ask the employee to provide their password or verification codes.

**Simulated finding:** The scenario did not establish the exact cause of the sign-in failure or confirm that the account had been compromised.

### Step 4 — Perform authorized account recovery

Following identity verification and authorization from the security team:

- Follow the organization's approved account-recovery procedure.
- Reset credentials if directed and appropriate.
- Revoke existing sessions if authorized and required by the incident-response procedure.
- Help the employee establish a secure authentication method through the approved process.
- Do not disable security controls merely to restore access.

**Simulated finding:** The scenario proceeds with authorized recovery actions. These are hypothetical actions, not changes made to a real account.

### Step 5 — Verify service restoration

- Confirm that the employee can sign in to Microsoft 365.
- Test Outlook and Teams access.
- Confirm the employee can continue their required work.
- If access is still failing, collect the remaining error details and escalate the unresolved problem.

**Simulated finding:** The employee successfully signs in, and Outlook and Teams appear to be working.

### Step 6 — Document and follow up

Record the following in the ticket:

- Reported symptoms and business impact.
- Timeline of unexpected MFA requests and sign-in failures.
- Identity-verification and investigation steps performed.
- Authorized recovery actions.
- Validation results for Outlook and Teams.
- Security-team involvement and the status of the related incident.
- Any remaining uncertainty, follow-up tasks, and escalation details.

Do not record passwords, MFA codes, or unnecessary sensitive information.

**Simulated finding:** Access is restored, but the precise cause of the unexpected MFA requests remains unconfirmed. The security incident must remain tracked according to organizational policy.

## 4. Example Troubleshooting Decision Flow

1. **Microsoft 365 sign-in fails:** Gather error messages and assess business impact.
2. **Unexpected MFA request reported:** Advise the employee to deny it and escalate the potential security incident.
3. **Investigation authorized:** Verify identity and review relevant sign-in evidence using approved tools.
4. **Recovery approved:** Follow authorized credential, session, and MFA recovery procedures.
5. **Access restored:** Verify Outlook and Teams with the employee.
6. **Cause remains uncertain:** Document the uncertainty and ensure security follow-up continues.

## 5. Security Best Practices

- Treat unexpected MFA requests as a potential security warning.
- Never approve a sign-in request that the employee did not initiate.
- Never request or store passwords or MFA verification codes in a ticket.
- Verify identity before performing account-recovery actions.
- Use least privilege and approved administrative procedures.
- Coordinate suspected account compromise with the security team.
- Do not claim a breach is confirmed without sufficient evidence.
- Keep the business impact, investigation status, and follow-up actions documented.
